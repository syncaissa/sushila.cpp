**TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) vs UDP (User Datagram Protocol)**
===========================================================

TCP and UDP are two fundamental protocols in the Internet Protocol Suite (TCP/IP) that enable data communication over the internet. While both protocols are used for data transfer, they have distinct differences in their approach, advantages, and use cases.

**TCP (Transmission Control Protocol)**
------------------------------------

TCP is a connection-oriented protocol, which means that a connection is established between the sender and receiver before data is sent. This connection is maintained throughout the data transfer process.

**Key characteristics:**

* **Reliable**: TCP ensures that data is delivered in the correct order and retransmits lost or corrupted packets.
* **Ordered**: TCP guarantees that packets are delivered in the order they were sent.
* **Error-checked**: TCP performs error-checking to detect and correct errors during transmission.
* **Connection-oriented**: A connection is established before data is sent.

**When to use TCP:**

* **File transfers**: TCP is ideal for transferring large files, such as documents, images, and videos, where reliability and order are crucial.
* **Web browsing**: TCP is used for HTTP (Hypertext Transfer Protocol) requests, ensuring that web pages are loaded correctly and in the right order.
