**TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) vs UDP (User Datagram Protocol)**

TCP and UDP are two fundamental transport-layer protocols used in the Internet Protocol Suite (TCP/IP). They are used to ensure reliable data transfer between devices on a network. While both protocols are used for data transfer, they have distinct differences in their approach, functionality, and use cases.

**TCP (Transmission Control Protocol)**

TCP is a connection-oriented protocol, which means it establishes a connection between the sender and receiver before data transfer begins. It ensures reliable data transfer by:

1.  **Sequence numbering**: Each packet is assigned a sequence number to ensure the correct order of packets.
2.  **Acknowledgment**: The receiver sends an acknowledgment (ACK) packet to the sender to confirm receipt of each packet.
3.  **Retransmission**: If a packet is lost or corrupted, the sender retransmits the packet.
4.  **Error detection**: TCP uses checksums to detect errors in the data.
