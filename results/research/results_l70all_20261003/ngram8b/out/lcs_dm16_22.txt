**TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) vs UDP (User Datagram Protocol)**

TCP and UDP are two fundamental transport-layer protocols used in the Internet Protocol Suite (TCP/IP). While both protocols enable data transfer between devices, they differ significantly in their approach to ensuring reliable data transfer.

**TCP (Transmission Control Protocol)**

* **Connection-oriented**: Establishes a connection between the sender and receiver before data transfer begins.
* **Reliable**: Ensures data is delivered in the correct order, with no duplication or loss.
* **Error-checked**: Verifies the integrity of data packets and requests retransmission if errors occur.
* **Flow control**: Regulates the amount of data sent to prevent network congestion.
* **Ordered delivery**: Guarantees that packets are delivered in the order they were sent.

**UDP (User Datagram Protocol)**

* **Connectionless**: Does not establish a connection before data transfer begins.
* **Unreliable**: Does not guarantee delivery, order, or integrity of data packets.
* **No error-checking**: Does not verify the integrity of data packets.
* **No flow control**: Does not regulate the amount of data sent.
* **Best-effort delivery**: Attempts to deliver packets, but may not guarantee delivery or order.
