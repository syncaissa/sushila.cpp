The process of how a bill becomes a law in the United States is as follows:

**Step 1: Introduction of the Bill**

- A member of Congress introduces a bill in either the House of Representatives or the Senate. The bill can be introduced by a single member or a group of members.
- The bill is then referred to a committee, which is a group of members responsible for reviewing and debating the bill.

**Step 2: Committee Review**

- The committee reviews the bill, holds hearings, and gathers information from experts and stakeholders.
- The committee may mark up the bill, which means they make changes to the bill before sending it to the full chamber for a vote.

**Step 3: Chamber Vote**

- The bill is sent to the full chamber (House of Representatives or Senate) for a vote.
- If a majority of the members present vote in favor of the bill, it passes and is sent to the other chamber (House or Senate) for consideration.

**Step 4: Conference Committee**

- If the bill passes in both chambers but is not identical, a conference committee is formed to iron out the differences between the two versions.
- The conference committee is made up of members from both chambers and is responsible for negotiating a compromise.
