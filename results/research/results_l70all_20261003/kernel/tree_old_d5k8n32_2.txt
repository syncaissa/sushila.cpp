**TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) vs UDP (User Datagram Protocol)**

TCP and UDP are two fundamental transport-layer protocols used in the Internet Protocol Suite (TCP/IP). While both protocols are used for data transfer, they have distinct differences in their design, functionality, and use cases.

**TCP (Transmission Control Protocol)**

* **Connection-oriented**: Establishes a connection between the sender and receiver before data transfer begins.
* **Reliable**: Ensures data is delivered in the correct order, with error-checking and retransmission of lost packets.
* **Ordered**: Guarantees that packets are delivered in the same order they were sent.
* **Flow control**: Regulates the amount of data sent to prevent network congestion.
* **Error detection**: Uses sequence numbers and acknowledgments to detect errors.

**UDP (User Datagram Protocol)**

* **Connectionless**: Does not establish a connection before data transfer begins.
* **Unreliable**: Does not guarantee delivery, and packets may be lost or arrive out of order.
* **Unordered**: Does not guarantee packet order, and packets may arrive in a different order than sent.
* **No flow control**: Does not regulate the amount of data sent, which can lead to network congestion.
