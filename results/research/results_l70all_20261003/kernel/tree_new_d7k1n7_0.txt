The process of how a bill becomes a law in the United States is as follows:

**Step 1: Introduction of the Bill**

- A member of Congress introduces a bill in either the House of Representatives or the Senate. The bill can be introduced by a single member or a group of members.
- The bill is then referred to a committee, which is a group of members responsible for reviewing and debating the bill.

**Step 2: Committee Review**

- The committee reviews the bill, holds hearings, and gathers information from experts and stakeholders.
- The committee may make changes to the bill or propose amendments.
- If the committee approves the bill, it is sent to the full chamber for consideration.

**Step 3: Chamber Debate and Vote**

- The bill is debated on the floor of the chamber where it was introduced.
- Members of Congress can propose amendments to the bill.
- The bill is then voted on by the members of the chamber. If a majority of the members vote in favor of the bill, it passes.

**Step 4: Referral to the Other Chamber**

- If the bill passes in one chamber, it is sent to the other chamber for consideration.
- The other chamber can approve the bill as is, reject it, or propose amendments
